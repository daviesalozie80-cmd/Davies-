# Your Niche Course: Global Economics × Geopolitics, Country by Country

**Your niche, in one sentence:** videos that explain why countries get richer or poorer, why life gets more or less affordable, and how money, debt, trade and power move between nations, told through maps, data and human stories.

## How this course works
1. Study the modules **in order**.
2. Each module ends with a **quiz with no answer key**. That's deliberate.
3. **Send me your answers in chat.** I'll mark them, explain any misunderstanding, give you a fresh example, and ask a follow-up question that is a bit harder.
4. Move on only when you score **8/10 or better**.

**Accuracy note:** facts in this course are historical and dated. Current numbers (debt totals, fertility rates, reserve shares) change, so the course shows you **where to check them** rather than asking you to trust a figure blindly. Anything marked **[debated]** or **[prediction]** isn't settled fact.

## PART 14: Your roadmap from beginner to content expert

### LEVEL 1: Beginner (weeks 1–2)
**Goal:** understand the basic meaning, language and concepts.
- Study Module 1 and pass Quiz 1.
- **Exercises:**
  1. Make flashcards for all 50 terms (front: the word; back: the simple meaning + an example).
  2. Each day, read one economics news headline and **translate it into plain English** in 2 sentences.
  3. Explain inflation, interest rates and government debt **out loud** to a friend, or record a voice note, without notes.
- **You're ready to move on when** you can read an FT or Reuters economics headline and explain it.

### LEVEL 2: Developing knowledge (weeks 3–5)
**Goal:** understand the categories, processes, history and players.
- Study Modules 2 and 3 and pass Quizzes 2–3.
- **Exercises:**
  1. Draw the 8 System diagrams (Module 2) from memory on paper.
  2. Build a **personal timeline** from 1944 to today with 25 events, and add one new event each week.
  3. Pick 3 countries and write a one-page "country profile" for each: GDP per person (PPP), debt-to-GDP, inflation, fertility rate, main exports, main trading partners, and one big problem. Use IMF, World Bank and OECD data only.
- **You're ready when** you can explain *why* a central bank raises rates and *who* holds a country's debt.

### LEVEL 3: Intermediate (weeks 6–9)
**Goal:** research, analyse and spot stories.
- Study Modules 4 and 5 and pass Quizzes 4–5.
- **Exercises:**
  1. Run the 15-minute daily topic routine for 21 days and build an idea bank of 60 or more ideas.
  2. For 5 ideas, build a full **fact sheet** (10–25 sourced numbers, each with two sources).
  3. Read one IMF *Article IV* country report summary and one OECD *Economic Survey* summary, and write a 200-word plain-English summary of each.
  4. Watch one competitor video and **fact-check 5 claims** against Tier 1 sources. Note any errors.
- **You're ready when** you can find a story nobody in your competitor list has covered, with a verified fact sheet.

### LEVEL 4: Advanced (weeks 10–14)
**Goal:** form independent, evidence-based views; identify gaps and developments.
- Study Module 6 and pass Quiz 6.
- **Exercises:**
  1. For 3 **[debated]** questions (e.g. "Is the dollar losing reserve status?"), write a 1-page brief: the evidence for, the evidence against, and your current view with a confidence level.
  2. Follow 3 recurring calendars: central bank decisions (Fed, ECB, BoE, BoJ), IMF WEO releases (April and October), and major elections. Before each event, predict the outcome; afterwards, review what you got right and wrong.
  3. Make a **gap list**: 20 countries or topics your competitors have ignored (use your competitor CSVs).
- **You're ready when** you can explain where experts disagree and why.

### LEVEL 5: Content expert (week 15 onward)
**Goal:** independently generate topics, verify, explain and script original videos.
- **Exercises:**
  1. Write 3 full scripts (about 2,400 words each) using the Module 5 structure. Every number must be in a fact sheet.
  2. Do a **"steelman" pass** on each: add the strongest counter-argument fairly. (*Steelman* = the strongest version of the other side's argument, the opposite of a strawman.)
  3. Have a knowledgeable friend, an economics subreddit or me review one script for accuracy and clarity.
  4. Publish, then compare the comments against your fact sheet and log any corrections.
- **You've arrived when** you can say: *"I can pick a country, find the real story, verify it, explain it simply, and defend it in the comments."*

## PART 15: How the testing works
- Each quiz goes from **Level 1 (recall)** up to **Level 4 (expert reasoning)**.
- **Answer in your own words.** Short answers are fine. Guessing is fine too, because mistakes show me what to re-teach.
- I'll reply with:
  1. a score
  2. what you got right
  3. a correction for each misunderstanding, explained differently from the module
  4. a new simple example
  5. one harder follow-up question
- I'll also keep a running list of your weak spots and revisit them in later quizzes.
