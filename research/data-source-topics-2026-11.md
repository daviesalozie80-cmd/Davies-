## How to mine these five sites yourself each week (about 30 minutes)

1. **IMF** ([WEO](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/weo), [DataMapper](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper)). Releases come in April and October, with July and January updates. Read the **press briefing and the "Key messages"**. Look for **concentration statistics, risk scenarios and the biggest forecast downgrades** by country.
2. **World Bank** ([data.worldbank.org](https://data.worldbank.org)). The *Global Economic Prospects* comes out in January and June, and the Commodity Markets Outlook in April and October. Use **Indicators** to chart one variable across countries (debt, remittances as % of GDP, fertility, electricity access). **The outlier country on the chart is your story.**
3. **OEC** ([oec.world](https://oec.world)). Open a country profile and read **"Latest Trends"** (monthly data). Look for **big year-on-year changes in one product or partner**, like Canada's gold. Then ask: is it real trade, or price and accounting?
4. **Harvard Atlas** ([atlas.hks.harvard.edu](https://atlas.hks.harvard.edu)). Check the **Growth Projections** ranking and the **"new products"** section of each country profile, which shows what a country recently learned to export. Rising complexity is the early sign of a "next economic miracle" story.
5. **UNCTAD** ([data stories](https://unctad.org/data-stories), [UNCTADstat insights](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/insights)). Short, chart-led pieces with a surprising number, for example **A World of Debt** (October), the World Investment Report (July), the *Review of Maritime Transport* (autumn), plus rapid crisis notes such as the Hormuz analyses.
6. **Then validate demand:** add the angle to `topic-queries.txt`, run `topic_scan.py`, and score the topic with the rubric before you commit to it.
