## 5. Scriptwriting and storytelling

**Caveat:** this is reconstructed. I could not access transcripts (see section 0).

### Likely structure [HYPOTHESIS]
The title and thumbnail promises, 16–20 minute runtimes and the map-heavy visuals point to the standard country-explainer arc:

| Section | Time | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold-open hook** | 0:00–0:30 | A shocking fact or scene that confirms the thumbnail ("33 countries are now telling citizens to keep food at home…"). Then the core question ("…so what do they know that we don't?") |
| **Stakes and promise** | 0:30–1:15 | Why it matters to you, plus a preview of the answer's surprising nature ("the answer isn't one disaster; it's five") |
| **Context and backstory** | 1–4 min | How the country or system got here. Map zoom-ins, timeline and a key chart |
| **Mechanism 1** | 4–8 min | The first cause, explained with one concrete example and one number |
| **Mid-video re-hook** | ~8 min | "But that's not the real problem…" (open loop) |
| **Mechanism 2 and 3 (rising tension)** | 8–14 min | Escalating causes, ending at the worst one |
| **Major revelation** | 14–17 min | The counter-intuitive twist: who benefits, why it can't be fixed, or the surprise winner |
| **Payoff and outlook** | 17–19 min | What happens next, with 2–3 scenarios |
| **Close** | final 30s | Brief takeaway and subscribe or sponsor message. Ends quickly to keep "end-of-video" retention |

### How to write original scripts with this formula
1. **Research first, write second.** Build a fact sheet of 15–25 sourced numbers from the IMF, World Bank, OECD, national statistics offices, FT/Reuters/Bloomberg and Economist reporting. Every claim gets a citation in your doc.
2. **Write your own thesis sentence** before drafting: "Country X's problem isn't A, it's B, and here's why that's worse."
3. **One idea per 60–90 seconds,** each with *claim → number → visual → consequence*.
4. **Plant an open loop every ~3 minutes:** "We'll come back to why that number matters."
5. **Never copy wording, order of arguments or unique examples** from the channel. Use their structure, not their sentences. Different sources and your own thesis make the script original by default.
6. Target **2,400–2,900 words** for a 17–19 minute video at about 150 wpm.

## 7. Thumbnail reverse-engineering

These observations come from the 16 top and 8 weak thumbnails in this folder [OBSERVED].

**The winning formula:**
1. **Background:** a 3D shaded-relief map in teal/cyan water and beige land, or a dark desaturated world map for global topics.
2. **Hero:** the subject country filled in **deep red** with a texture. On global topics, multiple countries glow red with **yellow arcs** connecting them.
3. **Text:** **2–5 words**, white bold condensed sans (Anton- or Bebas-like), each line in a **black box**. **One keyword is highlighted in yellow** ("NO FUTURE", "WORSE", "SCHEME").
4. **Narrative devices:**
   - Before/after split ("2016 → 2025", "RICH → POOR")
   - Arrows of flow (UK → Poland)
   - A crossed-out word ("GREECE FOR THE ~~GREEKS~~", "PORTUGAL FOR THE ~~PORTUGUESE~~")
   - Red ✕ markers
   - Fire around the US flag
5. **Faces:** used only on political or news hooks (Trump, Merz, Prabowo). They are **desaturated black-and-white**, worried or downcast, placed left, with the map or text on the right.
6. **Branding:** a circular "IH" badge in a top corner on most map thumbnails.
7. **Text never repeats the title.** The thumbnail delivers the emotion ("GAME OVER", "$1 TRILLION SCAM", "THIS ISN'T NORMAL"); the title delivers the topic.

**The losing pattern:** white or plain backgrounds, building photos, line charts, colour portraits with "WTF". This is generic news-channel styling that drops the brand recognisability.

### 10 original thumbnail concepts (same principles, your own visual identity)
Choose your own palette so you aren't a clone. Examples: deep navy sea with an amber subject country, or charcoal with a cyan subject.

1. **Netherlands:** country in amber on a navy sea, rising water texture. Text: "UNLIVABLE?" with "UN" highlighted.
2. **Global debt pensions:** world map with ageing countries glowing amber. Text: "WHO PAYS?"

## 8. Title and SEO strategy

**Observed rules [VERIFIED/OBSERVED]:**
- Titles are 6–10 words and about 44 characters, which fits on mobile.
- Common stems: **"Why…"** (43% of titles), "How…", "The Real Reason…", "What Happens if…", "Something's Going Seriously Wrong in…", "Why Living in X has Become Impossible", "Why X is Finally/Suddenly Becoming a Rich Country", "If Every Country…".
- Common power words: *Suddenly, Finally, Impossible, Insanely, Secretly, Real, Overdue, Collapse, Destroy, Seriously Wrong, Terrible, Nobody/No One.*
- Ellipses (…) create a two-beat structure with an open loop. The 5 ellipsis titles have a 2.3M median.
- Numbers are used rarely, and the titles that use them underperform ($1.5T military 65K; $37T crypto 78K).
- The style is browse-first, not search-first. Early titles were search-style ("The Problem with France's Economy | France Economics") and earned less. Since then titles are written for the home feed and suggested videos.
- **The channel keywords copy media brands** ("bloomberg", "the economist"). **[HYPOTHESIS]** This aims for adjacency to those channels' suggested-video traffic. Tags carry little weight today.
- **Descriptions and per-video tags:** not observable here (the page was blocked).
- **Shorts hashtags [VERIFIED]:** #economics #economy #geopolitics #geography #defense. Newer Shorts use emoji flags and no hashtags.

**The formula:** `[Why / What Happens If / If Every…] + [whole country or the world] + [emotional verb or state: impossible / destroying / suddenly rich / owed] + [optional "…" twist]`

## 9. Audience psychology and retention
(Inferred from structure; private retention data is unknown.)

- **Curiosity:** unanswered system questions ("who's it owed to?").
- **Threat and fear:** collapse, stockpiling, debt.
- **Validation and grievance:** "you're right, it's impossible to live here."
- **Status and identity:** your country vs. a rival ("UK's downfall making Poland rich").
- **Contrarian surprise:** "aid makes it poor."
- **Retention tools, standard for the format [HYPOTHESIS]:**
  - The answer is promised early and delivered late.
  - A new visual every few seconds: map zoom, chart build, label pop.
  - Re-hooks at mechanism transitions.
  - A "worst for last" cause order.
  - A short ending to avoid the outro drop-off.

**To apply it:**
- State the question in the first 10 seconds.
- Make a list promise ("three reasons, and the third explains everything").
- Re-hook every 2–3 minutes.
- Change visuals at least every 5 seconds.
- Pay off the title question explicitly in the last 15%.
- Then end within 20 seconds.

**Risks:**
- **Reused content:** press photos and news clips need fair-use discipline or licences. Map data is fine (Natural Earth is public domain).
- **Advertiser suitability:** war, migration and "collapse" topics can attract limited ads (yellow icons). The immigration videos (Denmark, Sweden) and the "migrant hotel" video are the most exposed.
- **Accuracy and reputation:** doom framing invites "fearmongering" criticism. Strong sourcing protects you.
- **AI voice and mass-produced content:** YouTube's inauthentic- and repetitious-content policies penalise templated, low-effort uploads. Human narration and original analysis are your insurance.

## 12. Replication strategy: your channel

## 14. Final verdict

1. **What makes it successful?** It packages macro-economics as personal, emotional country stories ("living there has become impossible"). It uses a consistent, instantly recognisable map-thumbnail brand and occasionally lands huge global-system mysteries.
2. **Biggest competitive advantage?** Visual brand consistency (the red country on a teal relief map, with black-box text) combined with emotionally framed titles.
3. **Most repeatable viral strategy?** The **"If Every Country… [mystery question]"** global-system format (3.0M and 2.3M), followed by **franchise template swaps** across countries ("Living in X Impossible": 7 videos, median 478K).
4. **Strongest content format?** A faceless 16–20 minute map documentary about one whole country or the whole world.
5. **Topic formula?** Personal stakes, plus a whole country or the world, plus a decline, reversal or mystery, plus mappability. A news peg is a bonus.
6. **Script formula (reconstructed)?** Shock fact → core question → stakes → backstory → escalating causes with re-hooks → counter-intuitive reveal → outlook → fast exit.
7. **Thumbnail formula?** A 3D map with the subject in red, 2–5 words in black boxes with one yellow keyword, a single device (arrow, split, strikethrough, ✕), and an optional black-and-white worried face on the left.
8. **Title formula?** `Why/What Happens If/If Every… + country or world + emotional state verb + optional "…" twist`, in about 8 words.
9. **How it holds attention?** By inference: a constant visual change of maps and charts, a delayed answer to the title question, and escalating causes.
10. **Is the niche worth entering?** **Yes, conditionally.** Demand is proven and RPM is strong. But it is crowded, so you need a distinct angle and visual identity, not a clone.
11. **Opportunities for a new creator:**
    - Uncovered countries and regions
    - A "what it means for your money" payoff
    - Optimistic "rising" stories
    - Scenario Shorts
    - Sourced credibility
    - Series packaging
12. **How I'd build a better version:**
    - Keep IH's packaging discipline.
    - Add a personal-finance payoff segment and visible sources.
    - Own a distinct palette and series names.
    - Cover the regions IH ignores.
    - Use Shorts as scenario teasers.
    - Run a strict outlier → sequel loop every month.
