# GEOECONOMICS EXPLAINED: Topic Research Report
**Research date:** 18 November 2026

**Supporting files in this folder**
- `topic-evidence-2026-11-18.csv`: 144 videos uploaded this month across 30 searches, with views, subscribers and outlier ratios.
- `topic_scan.py`: re-runs the scan.
- `topic-queries.txt`: starter search list.

## How this was researched (and the limits)
- **News (Part 1):** live web searches on 18 Nov 2026, with sources linked. Where sources disagreed on figures, I say so.
- **YouTube demand (Parts 2–3):** 30 YouTube searches restricted to **videos uploaded this month** (YouTube's own filter), plus the latest uploads of 10 competitor channels.
  - **View counts are real, taken 18 Nov.**
  - Upload dates come from YouTube's "X days/weeks ago" labels. They're exact to the day for recent videos, but anything shown as "1 month ago" is approximate (shown as ~).
- **Outlier test.** A video is a demand signal when either:
  - **views ÷ subscribers** is high, meaning it travelled far beyond the channel's own audience; or
  - **views ÷ that channel's median video** is ≥ 3×.

  A topic counts as **validated** when ≥ 2 independent channels show it.
- **Not available here:** Google Trends and YouTube autocomplete are blocked in this environment. **No search-volume or Trends figures appear in this report.** Run Trends yourself as an extra check (section 10).
- **Labels:** **[CONFIRMED]** = a reported event. **[FORECAST]** = a projection. **[UNVERIFIED]** = a single or weak source.

## 10. Your repeatable weekly research system
1. **Monday (45 min): news scan.**
   - Sources: Reuters/FT/Bloomberg economics pages, IEA/EIA/IMF/FAO releases, central bank calendars.
   - List 10 developments in the Part 1 table format.
2. **Monday (20 min): demand scan.**
   - Add the 10 developments as queries to `topic-queries.txt`.
   - Run `python3 research/topic_scan.py research/topic-queries.txt > research/scan-YYYY-MM-DD.csv`.
3. **Filter:** keep topics with
   - **any video ≥ 10× its channel's subscribers, or ≥ 3× its channel's median**, AND
   - **≥ 2 independent channels**, OR a strong outlier plus a clear news peg.
4. **Saturation check:** if 3+ channels over 250K subscribers covered the topic in the last 30 days, choose a different angle (winners, second-order effect, history mirror) or skip.
5. **Google Trends check (manual):**
   - Compare the topic against a stable benchmark term over 90 days. Look for **rising relative interest** and note related queries.
   - Trends shows relative interest, not volume. Never quote it as a number of searches.
6. **Verify:** confirm every key number with a primary source (Module 5 research process) before scoring.
7. **Score** with the 10-criterion rubric (out of 100):
   - ≥ 80: produce now
   - 70–79: research further
   - 60–69: save
   - under 60: reject
8. **Monthly review:** compare your own videos' click-through rate and retention against the score you gave them, and adjust the rubric weights.
