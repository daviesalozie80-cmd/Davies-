## 1. Title formulas and thumbnail styles that perform in history/prehistory/"ancient humans" content

### Takeaway
The best-performing prehistory titles use four patterns. Each poses an open question that viewers can only answer by clicking:
- **Second-person survival or immersion:** "How long would YOU last...", "Your time machine broke..."
- **Scale or mystery paradox:** "survived 2 million years – scientists can't explain how", "300,000 years doing almost nothing"
- **Daily-life curiosity:** "What did ancient humans do all day..."
- **Sweeping compression:** "All of human history in one hour"

General packaging rules from top strategists, including Paddy Galloway, are: titles under about 60 characters, under 5 words of thumbnail text, no more than 3 focal points, a title and thumbnail that complement rather than repeat each other, and 10+ title options per video. YouTube's native A/B tool now picks winners by watch-time share, not by CTR.

**Packaging rules (Paddy Galloway, whose work spans more than 50B views)**
- Writes 10+ titles and at least 3 distinct thumbnails per video, then tests them with YouTube's native A/B tool. Treats packaging as about half of a video's success. Titles under about 60 characters. Thumbnail text under 5 words, with no more than 3 focal areas. — [Creatorpoint summary](https://creatorpoint.co/how-to-go-viral-on-youtube-paddy-galloway/); [onlyhappynews summary](https://onlyhappynews.com/happy-studio/mr-beasts-secret-youtube-consultant-has-worked-on-50-billion-views-here-are-his-actual-rules/)
- "Make sure thumbnail passes the 'glance test'... view the title and thumbnail as one, do they compliment each other or contradict/repeat?" — [Paddy Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1811083499044044951)
- The title and thumbnail should pose a question that can only be answered by clicking. He favours a "paused in action" thumbnail over posed imagery. — [Creatorpoint](https://creatorpoint.co/how-to-go-viral-on-youtube-paddy-galloway/)

**YouTube Test & Compare**
- YouTube expanded Test & Compare from thumbnails to titles as an experiment in July 2025. Thumbnail A/B testing was announced in 2023 and rolled out from 2024. — [Tubefilter, Jul 16 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/16/youtube-feature-test-and-compare-titles-thumbnails/)
- Up to 3 variants can be tested. The winner is chosen by watch-time share, not CTR. Third-party guides say title testing reached all eligible creators globally in December 2025. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-title-a-b-testing-rolls-out-globally-to-creators); [thumbnailtest.com](https://thumbnailtest.com/news/youtube-releases-title-test-compare/)

### Inferences
- **Title formula bank** distilled from the examples above:
  1. "How long would you survive in [era]?"
  2. "You wake up in [30,000 BC]. Here's what happens."
  3. "What did [ancient humans] actually do all day?"
  4. "Why did humans [do nothing for 300,000 years]?"
  5. "[Species/group] survived [X] years. Scientists can't explain how."
  6. "The [surprising behaviour] of hunter-gatherers (that we lost)"
  7. "All of [topic] in one hour"
- A Boehm/Wiessner/Lee-style script on egalitarianism, leisure and fire-side talk maps naturally onto pattern 3. One option: "What did ancient humans do at night?", which ties to Wiessner's firelight-conversation research. Another: "Why hunter-gatherers worked less than you", which ties to Richard Lee's !Kung data.
- Because Test & Compare optimises for watch-time share, a clickbaity title that loses viewers will lose the test. Packaging must be honest to the content.
- **Thumbnail recipe for faceless prehistory:** one striking illustrated figure or scene, high contrast, 0–4 words, plus one curiosity element (a hand stencil, a fire at night, a skull with a question). Kurzgesagt and Fossil-style survival videos lean on a clean illustrated style and a single focal subject.

## 2. Hook and retention in the first 30 seconds; ideal length for new channels

### Takeaway
YouTube's "Intro" metric is the share of viewers still watching at 30 seconds. Creator-tool benchmarks put a good hook at roughly 50–70% retained at 30 seconds, with about 60% or more as a target. Every source names the same causes of early exits: a mismatch between packaging and opening, slow or branded intros, and an unclear payoff. On length, successful prehistory and history videos span 10–60 minutes. A recent AI-history success, Chloe VS History, reportedly grew after moving into long-form documentaries.

### Cited Findings
- **Benchmarks:**
  - The Intro metric is the percentage of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds, effectively a "hook score". Guides suggest aiming for 60%+ at 30 seconds; other guides call 50–70% solid for longer videos. — [vidIQ retention guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/); [Opus retention graphs](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-retention-graphs-explained)
  - Estimates of how many viewers leave within 30 seconds conflict (30–40% vs 50–70%). No primary study was found. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/); [mean.ceo hook stats](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11560)
- **Causes of early exits:** a title/thumbnail that doesn't match the opening, slow or branded intros, and an unclear payoff. — [async.com](https://async.com/blog/why-videos-lose-engagement/); [Opus](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-retention-graphs-explained)
- **Length evidence from the niche:**
  - Kurzgesagt "Human Origins" runs 9m39s (23.8M views). — [rosetta.to](https://rosetta.to/u/kurzgesagt/what-happened-before-history-human-origins)
  - Kurzgesagt "Time Machine" runs 12m53s (5.7M). — [rosetta.to](https://rosetta.to/u/kurzgesagt/your-time-machine-broke-at-the-worst-time-in-history)
  - WIRED's Q&A runs 17m (5.5M). — [rosetta.to](https://rosetta.to/u/wired/paleoanthropologist-answers-caveman-questions-from-twitter-tech-support-wired)
  - The Why Files runs 46m (6.4M). — [rosetta.to](https://rosetta.to/u/thewhyfiles/humans-vs-superhumans-when-monsters-were-real-and-we-almost-went-extinct)
  - Kurzgesagt's 1-hour video drew 2.8M. — [rosetta.to](https://rosetta.to/u/kurzgesagt/all-of-human-history-in-one-hour)
- **Chloe VS History length:** one write-up says the channel took off after it switched to 15–20 minute videos. — [The Blueprint AI Substack](https://theblueprintai.substack.com/p/start-a-faceless-youtube-channel) Other coverage says only that the creator shifted from short-form posts to long-form YouTube documentaries, and gives no run time. — [The Executive Brief](https://the-executive-brief.web.app/news/jonathan-laramy-built-chloe-vs-history-from-scratch-then-cracked-long-form-youtube-revenue-a9916d)
- **Sleep-history videos** run roughly 2–5 hours, with several uploads a week. — [lilys.ai note on "Boring History" channel](https://lilys.ai/en/notes/ai-automated-income-20260203/ai-youtube-channel-100k-90-days); [mypodcastdata episode list](https://www.mypodcastdata.com/podcast/show/boring-history-for-sleep-velvet-r7y/episodes)

### Inferences
- **Hook template for a prehistory explainer (first 30 seconds):**
  - 0–5s: restate the title's promise visually ("It's 30,000 BC. The sun has just gone down.").
  - 5–15s: a counter-intuitive claim with stakes ("And what these people did next is the reason you can read this sentence.").
  - 15–30s: a roadmap or open loop ("By the end, you'll see why anthropologists think...").
  - No logo intro and no "welcome back".
- **Length for a new channel:** 10–20 minutes is the best-supported sweet spot. It is long enough for watch time and mid-roll ads (8+ minutes) and short enough for a new narrator to hold retention. Test one 30–60 minute "compilation/deep dive" around days 60–90, once 8–12 single topics exist that can be stitched together.

### Gaps
- No primary YouTube data on optimal length for new education/history channels.
- A claim that AI narration causes about 35% higher drop-off in the first 45 seconds than a human voice appeared in vendor blog search results, but I could not trace or verify the underlying study.

## 3. Visual styles for faceless channels (illustrated/animated, stock, AI images): trade-offs for trust and policy

### Takeaway
The biggest prehistory hits use illustration or animation: Kurzgesagt, Fossil and Hominid History Hub's niche. AI-image and AI-voice history is growing fast and can scale (Chloe VS History has about 421K subscribers), but it faces press and historian backlash over inaccuracy. It also carries monetization risk under YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" policy, which targets mass-produced or repetitive videos, not AI use itself. The safest route is human-written, research-led scripts with a consistent visual identity. AI visuals are acceptable as long as they are not templated slop.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube policy (July 15, 2025):**
  - YouTube renamed the YPP "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content" and described it as a minor clarification targeting mass-produced or highly repetitive videos. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)
  - YouTube said authentic content made with AI tools remains acceptable. The rule governs monetization eligibility, not whether videos may stay on the platform. — [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/de/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/)
  - Press coverage, not YouTube, listed likely targets: AI voices over templates, near-duplicates and low-effort compilations. — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/youtube-ramps-up-campaign-against-ai-slop-with-stricter-monetization-caveats-on-inauthentic-and-repetitive-videos)
- **Backlash over AI "boring history":**
  - 404 Media reported a network of AI-narrated "boring history" channels (Sleepless Historian, Boring History Bites) filling the YouTube homepage. A quoted historian said they recycle simplified online material. — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/ai-generated-boring-history-videos-are-flooding-youtube-and-drowning-out-real-history/)
  - Historians called AI history videos "amateur and dangerous". — [madcornishprojectionist (reprint)](https://www.madcornishprojectionist.co.uk/amateur-and-dangerous-historians-weigh-in-on-viral-ai-history-videos/)
  - SVT cited a Kapwing report that more than 20% of content recommended to new YouTube users is "AI slop". Historian-YouTuber Pete Kelly said such videos have "absolutely no quality control". — [SVT](https://www.svt.se/kultur/ai-genererade-historievideos-tar-over-youtube)
  - Independent debunkers now publish reviews of AI history channels, e.g. "Time Witnessed" (June 2026). — [Fake History Hunter](https://fakehistoryhunter.net/2026/06/06/ai-review-time-witnessed/)
- **AI can still win when crafted (Chloe VS History):**
  - The channel has an AI-generated time-travelling host. Creator Jonathan Laramy quit a customer-service job in June 2025 to run it. — [Business Insider Japan](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2606-quit-job-now-makes-ai-videos-youtube-earns-more-money/)
  - Each video costs £300–800 to make, mostly from repeated AI generation. — [Business Insider Japan](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2606-quit-job-now-makes-ai-videos-youtube-earns-more-money/)
  - Social Blade lists about 421K subscribers, 32M views and 64 videos. — [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/chloevshistory/similar)
  - It reportedly reached 261K subscribers in its first 3 months. — [ebizfacts](https://ebizfacts.com/?p=3952667)
  - It has 610K+ Instagram followers. — [The Cool Down](https://www.thecooldown.com/green-business/chloe-history-influencer-ai-reveal/)
  - Laramy says "having a personality helps on social media". — [The Cool Down](https://www.thecooldown.com/green-business/chloe-history-influencer-ai-reveal/)
  - There was public debate when audiences learned the host was AI. — [Hola](https://www.hola.com/us/lifestyle/20260626909651/ai-history-influencer-tiktok-debate/)
- **Faceless creators adapting:** The Hollywood Reporter says faceless creators are mixing in on-camera narration since algorithm changes appear to reward human faces. — [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/digital/faceless-creators-youtube-ai-damage-1236617586/)

### Inferences
- **Trade-off summary:**
  - Custom illustration or animation gives the highest trust and brand value, at the highest cost and slowest output.
  - Stock footage is cheap, but viewers flag it as generic, and Paleolithic subject matter has little good stock.
  - AI images are fast and can show prehistoric scenes that do not exist, but they carry the "slop" stigma, a risk of anachronism (wrong tools, clothing and faces that experts call out), and a weak spot under the inauthentic-content rule if templated.
- **Recommendation:** a hybrid. Keep a consistent art style, whether AI-assisted with heavy curation or illustrated. Show real artefacts and site photos (Chauvet, Blombos, Ju/'hoansi ethnographic stills where licensing allows). Add on-screen citations. Use a human or carefully directed voice. Disclose realistic synthetic content as YouTube requires.

- I did not verify YouTube's altered/synthetic content disclosure rules in this session.

## 4. Upload cadence and the role of Shorts/TikTok/Reels

### Takeaway
Early cadence evidence comes mostly from vendor datasets, not from YouTube. The data says Shorts bring views and long-form brings subscribers: about 505 Shorts views per subscriber gained, versus far fewer views per subscriber for long-form. Channels with a mixed format mix reached 1,000 subscribers faster, at a median of 108 uploads. Long-form-only channels had more subscribers in one 18,000-channel correlational study. Cross-posting clips to TikTok and Instagram fuelled Chloe VS History's early reach.

### Cited Findings
- **vidIQ study of 200,000 channels created in 2025:**
  - Shorts needed about 505 views per subscriber gained.
  - Long-form converted far better. The search summary stated "106 subscribers per view", which is likely a garbling of about 106 views per subscriber; unverified.
  - Channels with a mixed upload mix reached 1,000 subscribers faster, at a median of 108 uploads.
  - Summary line: "Shorts get you views and long form gets you subscribers."
  - [vidIQ via rosetta.to, "The Fastest Way to 1k Subs in 2026"](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/the-fastest-way-to-1k-subs-in-2026); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-growth)
- **AIR Media-Tech, 18,000 channels (Mar 2025–Mar 2026):**
  - Long-form-only gaming channels had 85% more subscribers than channels that also posted Shorts.
  - The authors flag this as correlational. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)
- **Shorts funnel mechanics:**
  - A Short with 10–20K views may send only about 50 views to long-form. One with 10M views can send 3,000+.
  - A pinned comment linking to the long-form video reportedly converts at 1–3%, better than the "related video" link.
  - [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/drive-traffic-shorts-to-long-videos)
- **Adobe Express survey of 507 creators (1K+ subscribers):** 49% said Shorts under 30 seconds drive more click-throughs to long-form. — [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/youtube-shorts-length-study/)
- **Cadence anecdotes:**
  - The "Boring History" AI channel posted daily, 2–3 hour videos, with repeatable title, thumbnail and script templates. Earnings claims are garbled and unverified. — [lilys.ai](https://lilys.ai/en/notes/ai-automated-income-20260203/ai-youtube-channel-100k-90-days)
  - One FacelessIndex case study reached 1,000 subscribers in month 3 with daily publishing. — [FacelessIndex](https://facelessindex.com/case-studies)
  - Chloe VS History had only 64 videos at about 421K subscribers, which suggests quality over volume. — [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/chloevshistory/similar)
  - Hominid History Hub had 148 videos at about 415K subscribers. — [subsub.io](https://open.subsub.io/channels/hominidhistoryhub/view)
- **Cross-posting:** Chloe VS History built its following on short-form social (Instagram 610K+) before shifting to long-form YouTube documentaries. — [The Executive Brief](https://the-executive-brief.web.app/news/jonathan-laramy-built-chloe-vs-history-from-scratch-then-cracked-long-form-youtube-revenue-a9916d); [The Cool Down](https://www.thecooldown.com/green-business/chloe-history-influencer-ai-reveal/)

### Inferences
- **90-day cadence suggestion for a solo, research-led channel:**
  - 1–2 long-form videos (10–20 minutes) per week, about 15–20 over 90 days.
  - 3–5 Shorts per week cut from each script's single most surprising fact, under 30 seconds, with a pinned comment linking to the long-form video.
  - The same vertical clips cross-posted to TikTok and Reels.
- Daily output is how the AI-slop channels operate, and it is the exact profile the inauthentic-content policy targets. Large channels in this niche succeeded with fewer, stronger videos.

- I could not fetch the vidIQ or AIR pages to confirm exact figures. Upload cadence in the first 90 days of Hominid History Hub or the Fossil channel is unknown.

## 5. Credibility: citing real studies (Boehm, Wiessner, Lee) and comment engagement

### Takeaway
I found no direct data showing that citing studies lifts views. However, 2025–2026 coverage frames accuracy and "real research" as the main thing separating legitimate history channels from AI slop, in the eyes of the press, historians, debunkers and YouTube's own monetization policy. Named scholars and on-screen citations work as a trust signal and as protection for monetization.

### Cited Findings
- **The press frames the problem as missing quality control and analysis:**
  - "no analysis of historical events and absolutely no quality control" (Pete Kelly). — [SVT](https://www.svt.se/kultur/ai-genererade-historievideos-tar-over-youtube)
  - AI history videos repeat simplified online material. — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/ai-generated-boring-history-videos-are-flooding-youtube-and-drowning-out-real-history/)
- **Original research and monetization:** a faceless history or true-crime channel "with its own script, its own narration, and its own research is monetized like any other". — [OutlierKit on demonetization](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-youtube-channel-demonetized/)
- **The quality bar:** Kurzgesagt, the benchmark for prehistory explainers (23.8M views on "Human Origins"), is known for publishing sources. That reputation is general knowledge; this session did not re-verify it. — [rosetta.to](https://rosetta.to/u/kurzgesagt/what-happened-before-history-human-origins)
- **Expert Q&A appeal:** WIRED's "Paleoanthropologist Answers Caveman Questions" has 5.5M views, which shows strong demand for expert-authority framing in this topic. — [rosetta.to](https://rosetta.to/u/wired/paleoanthropologist-answers-caveman-questions-from-twitter-tech-support-wired)

### Inferences
- **Use named researchers as story beats, not as footnotes.** For example: "In the 1960s, anthropologist Richard Lee lived with the !Kung and timed their work..." or "Polly Wiessner recorded what people talked about by firelight versus daylight..." Use Boehm's "reverse dominance hierarchy" as a payoff reveal.
- **Put sources in the description, with timestamps.** Show the paper or book cover on screen. This defends against the "AI slop" label and makes debunkers less likely to target the channel.
- **Comment tactics.** Ask one specific, opinion-forcing question at the payoff, not at the end. Example: "Would you trade your job for a 20-hour hunter-gatherer week — and what would you miss?" Pin a comment with the question plus a source link. Reply to the first hour of comments.
- The Chloe VS History reveal debate shows that undisclosed AI personas create a trust risk. A clear disclosure of narration and visual methods avoids that.

### Gaps
- No A/B or correlational data was found showing that citations or named scholars raise CTR, retention or comment rates.
- No data was found on the effect of comment prompts on growth in this niche.

## 6. Common reasons new faceless channels fail

### Takeaway
The recurring failure modes are:
- no distinct voice or personality;
- generic stock footage and robotic narration;
- templated, mass-produced output, which now also brings demonetization risk;
- weak packaging;
- unrealistic "get rich quick" expectations, leading creators to quit within about 90 days;
- topics that are too broad.

These sources are mostly vendor blogs, so they show common themes rather than rigorous statistics.

### Cited Findings
- Faceless videos often fail for lack of a distinctive personality. Channels fail when they attempt personality-driven formats without the personality. — [Subscribr: mistakes new faceless AI channels make](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/mistakes-new-faceless-ai-channels); [kineclip](https://kineclip.com/blog/why-faceless-youtube-channels-fail/)
- Viewers quickly recognise generic, repetitive stock footage and robotic narration. — [easyviral](https://easyviral.ai/blog/why-90-percent-faceless-youtube-channels-fail-2026)
- Many people treat faceless YouTube as an easy way to get rich quick. One source claims most new faceless channels fail within 90 days, but that figure is unsourced. — [Medium](https://medium.com/@kimutail/why-99-of-faceless-youtube-channels-fail-da2a4ee649d3); [tech4ssd](https://tech4ssd.beehiiv.com/p/the-real-reason-faceless-youtube-channels-fail-how-to-fix-it-b174)
- Specific sub-niches still work (e.g. a channel devoted entirely to WWII). Broad "history" is crowded. — [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/digital/faceless-creators-youtube-ai-damage-1236617586/)
- The sleep-history niche is competitive, but narrow sub-niches (e.g. "Victorian crime", "medieval sanitation") are under-served. — [Autonolab sleep niche](https://blog.autonolab.com/niches/2026-02-06-faceless-youtube-sleep/)
- Monetization risk: repetitive or templated AI content falls under the YPP "inauthentic content" rule. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/); [Vizard](https://vizard.ai/blog/youtubes-crackdown-on-ai-slop-is-reshaping-the-faceless-creator-economy)
- A channel not growing after 20 videos is usually down to packaging and topic selection, not upload volume. This is the framing of a vendor article. — [vidrush](https://vidrush.ai/blog/faceless-youtube-channel-not-growing)

### Inferences
- **Risks for an "ancient humans / anthropology" launch:**
