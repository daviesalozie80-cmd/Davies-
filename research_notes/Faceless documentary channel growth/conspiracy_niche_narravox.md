# Faceless conspiracy documentaries by world location, and the AI tool Narravox (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: research ran on 2026-11-16. WebFetch could not reach any of the primary pages: narravoxai.com, youtube.com, devpost.com and support.google.com all failed DNS because they are outside the session's network allowlist. **Nearly every figure below therefore comes from search-engine snippets or summaries, not from the full pages.** Subscriber counts come from third-party trackers (vidIQ, Social Blade, Socialcounts, CreatorDB) and are estimates. RPM figures come mostly from vendor blogs that sell AI or faceless-channel tools.

## 1. Leading conspiracy and mystery documentary channels: size, age, growth, and fast-growing new faceless channels

### Takeaway
The leading channels are large (1.4M to 6M subscribers), built by named hosts, and many years old. None is a new AI-voiced faceless channel. I found no verifiable 2024–2026 case of a new faceless conspiracy channel reaching 10k–100k+ within months. The fast-growth claims I found are unverified, unnamed, or from sales pages.

### Cited Findings
- **The Why Files** (host AJ Gentile, so not faceless): about 5.89M subscribers in July 2026. Another snapshot puts it at 5.99M subscribers, 518 videos and about 1.28B views on 2026-09-30. Trackers imply roughly +370k subscribers from January to July 2026. Snippets say it had only about 5,000 subscribers in its first year and later broke through with Shorts/TikTok clips and then long-form videos. (Snippets only.) — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/thewhyfiles/); [Builtbyfoundry case study](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/aj-gentile-why-files-6m-subscribers-334-book-reading-list); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCIFk2uvCNcEmZ77g0ESKLcQ)
- A 2023 Reddit thread questioned a sudden surge for The Why Files, from a few hundred subscribers to several hundred thousand within weeks. This is anecdotal. — [r/TheWhyFiles thread (mirror)](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/TheWhyFiles/comments/1563nhw/the_mysterious_rise_of_why_files_is_it_too_good)

### Inferences
- The top tier rewards long research cycles, distinctive authored voices and multi-year compounding. LEMMiNO and Barely Sociable post rarely, with very high production value. A new AI-voiced channel competes for the same viewers against these benchmarks.
- The Why Files' trajectory (about 5k subscribers in year one, then a breakthrough) suggests that even successful channels in this niche usually do not explode within 3 months.
- Reaching 10k subscribers in 3 months is plausible if one video breaks out. Reaching 100k+ in 3 months would be an outlier, and I found no verified conspiracy-niche precedent for it.

### Gaps
- Channel start dates were not confirmed from primary pages. My background knowledge puts LEMMiNO at roughly 2010s, Wendigoon and Nexpo at around 2017–2020, The Why Files at about 2021, and Barely Sociable at about 2018, but all of these are unverified here.
- I found no named, verifiable new faceless AI conspiracy channel from 2024–2026 with a public growth curve. Social Blade's "top new channels" lists could not be fetched.

## 2. Popular topics and formats, location-based series, and underserved regions

### Takeaway
The dominant formats are the deep-dive documentary (The Why Files, LEMMiNO, Barely Sociable) and the "iceberg" tiered explainer that Wendigoon popularized. I found no established "conspiracy from every continent" or country-by-country series. That suggests an open format niche, but it is also unproven demand.

### Cited Findings
- Wendigoon's Conspiracy Theory Iceberg is a 21-part series breaking down a 10-level 4chan iceberg, with over 16M views. Icebergs rank theories by public awareness, and spin-offs include serial-killer and religion/cult icebergs. — [Rosetta (Wendigoon)](https://rosetta.to/u/wendigoon/the-conspiracy-theory-iceberg); [TheTVDB](https://www.thetvdb.com/series/wendigoon/seasons/official/2021)
- Press coverage profiles a young YouTuber "sharing conspiracy theories responsibly", which frames responsible handling as part of the appeal. — [AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/meet-youtuber-sharing-conspiracy-theories-153131462.html)
- A search for "every continent" or "every country" conspiracy series returned no matching established series. — (negative search result; see [search context](https://rosetta.to/u/wendigoon/page/2))
- Vendor guides recommend narrative-driven, less controversial mysteries and say "conspiracy theories explained" and debunking sub-niches are growing. One calls debunking "still wide open". — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/blog/is-conspiracy-theories-a-good-youtube-niche-in-2026-rpm-and-growth-data); [FluxNote debunking guide](https://fluxnote.io/guides/make-money-conspiracy-debunking-youtube-channel)
- Investigative-journalism framing is said to attract a more educated, higher-RPM audience. This is a vendor claim. — [FluxNote dark history](https://fluxnote.io/guides/dark-history-secrets-channel)

### Inferences
- A continent-by-continent structure is a differentiated format that works as a playlist. African, Asian and South American true-life cover-ups and conspiracies (for example, state cover-ups, Cold War operations and colonial-era incidents) look less covered by the large English channels than US and European topics. That is an inference; I did not measure it.
- Underserved topics carry two risks: less English-language source material, so more fact-checking load, and potentially lower search demand from high-RPM US and UK viewers.

### Gaps
- I have no keyword-volume data (vidIQ, Google Trends) for region-specific conspiracy topics.
- I found no data on how African, Asian or South American conspiracy videos perform relative to US-centred ones.

## 3. How YouTube treats conspiracy content: recommendations, misinformation, info panels and ad-suitability

### Takeaway
Since January 2019 YouTube has reduced recommendations of "borderline" content, meaning content that misleads in harmful ways (flat earth, miracle cures, false claims about historic events) without breaking its rules. It also restricts ads on sensitive material. Channels that stay monetized and recommended frame their content as evidence-based, "what really happened", or debunking. I found no 2025–2026 public update on how borderline demotion is enforced now.

### Cited Findings
- January 2019: YouTube said it would reduce recommendations of borderline content that "could misinform users in harmful ways". Its examples were phony miracle cures, flat-earth claims and blatantly false claims about historic events like 9/11. — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/continuing-our-work-to-improve); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2019/01/25/youtube-recommendations-conspiracy-videos-google-pledges-tweak-them)
- The 2019 change affected less than 1% of videos and started in the US. Videos stay up and remain reachable through search and subscriptions. — [Tech.co](https://tech.co/?p=313566); [Videomaker](https://www.videomaker.com/news/youtube-promises-itll-stop-recommending-conspiracy-videos/)
- YouTube later said that views of borderline content coming from recommendations were "significantly below 1%", and that a US test cut recommendation views of such content by more than 50%. (Older data, around 2019–2020.) — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=165428); [Reclaim The Net](https://reclaimthenet.org/?p=14957)
- An academic audit (University of Amsterdam) found that bots primed with conspiracy videos were quickly recommended much more conspiracy content. In other words, personalization can still amplify the genre for viewers who already watch it. — [UvA paper](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/234176347/Accounting_for_Personalization_in_Personalization_Algorithms.pdf)
- **Ad-suitability:** in January 2026 YouTube expanded full monetization to "controversial issues" content when it is non-graphic or dramatized. This follows a 2023 relaxation covering abuse, abortion and eating disorders. — [TechCrunch 2026-01-16](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/16/youtube-relaxes-monetization-guidelines-for-some-controversial-topics/); [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/26/youtube-relaxes-advertiser-friendly-guidelines-around-controversial-topics-like-abortion-abuse-and-eating-disorders); [WTOP](https://wtop.com/business-finance/2026/01/youtube-relaxes-monetization-policy-on-videos-with-controversial-content/)
- A low-reliability monetization-tips site lists conspiracy theories, "even debunked ones", among flagged categories. I could not confirm this against YouTube's primary guideline text (support.google.com was blocked). — [info-youtube-monetization-tips](https://info-youtube-monetization-tips.pages.dev/post/what-youtubes-advertiser-friendly-guidelines-really-mean)
- A 2020 Quartz analysis found that advertiser rules are broader than the community guidelines on harmful content. YouTube disputed it. — [Quartz](https://qz.com/1785613/how-youtube-shields-advertisers-not-viewers-from-harmful-videos)
- Vendor guidance warns that content promoting dangerous theories risks demonetization or termination, and recommends narrative mystery framing instead. — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/blog/is-conspiracy-theories-a-good-youtube-niche-in-2026-rpm-and-growth-data)

### Inferences
- "True-life conspiracy events" means documented operations and cover-ups (for example, declassified programs or proven state deceptions). This is the safest lane: it is history or journalism, not borderline misinformation. Promoting unproven theories (health, elections, 9/11 trutherism, anti-vaccine) is where demotion, information panels and limited ads apply.
- Violent or graphic depictions (massacres, assassinations) can still trigger limited ads under the "violence" and "shocking" categories even when the content is factual. The January 2026 relaxation helps non-graphic, dramatized treatments.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve YouTube's current misinformation-policy text, the information-panel policy text (panels appear on topics prone to misinformation, such as the moon landing and 9/11, per my background knowledge; unverified here), or the advertiser-friendly guidelines.
- I found no 2024–2026 public data on whether borderline demotion has been tightened or loosened.

## 4. RPM and CPM for conspiracy and mystery content

### Takeaway
Estimates for conspiracy content cluster at roughly $2–8 RPM. Mystery, dark-history and documentary framing reportedly reaches about $4–15. All of these are vendor estimates, not verified data.

### Cited Findings
- Conspiracy: about $3.00–8.50 RPM (FluxNote 2026) and $3–7 RPM (Becomeviral). — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/blog/is-conspiracy-theories-a-good-youtube-niche-in-2026-rpm-and-growth-data); [Becomeviral](https://becomeviral.com/blog/conspiracy-niche-youtube.html)
- Conspiracy CPM $2–6, limited by advertiser sensitivity; debunking CPM $2–5. — [FluxNote debunking](https://fluxnote.io/guides/make-money-conspiracy-debunking-youtube-channel)
- Unsolved mysteries $4–9 RPM; mystery CPM $6–12; secret-societies CPM $8–15; dark history $5–10 RPM. — [FluxNote mystery](https://fluxnote.io/blog/is-mystery-a-good-youtube-niche-in-2026-rpm-and-growth-data); [FluxNote secret societies](https://fluxnote.io/guides/make-money-secret-societies-youtube-channel); [FluxNote dark history](https://fluxnote.io/guides/dark-history-secrets-channel)
- An audit of 31 faceless documentary channels reported an average RPM of $12.6 and "medium saturation". The sample is small and the source is a vendor. — [Autonolab](https://blog.autonolab.com/niches/2025-11-29-faceless-youtube-documentary/)
- OutlierKit notes that YouTube publishes no universal niche RPM benchmark, and that it revised its own page to remove such figures. — [OutlierKit RPM benchmarks](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-rpm-benchmarks/)

### Inferences
- Framing the channel as "documentary or history" rather than "conspiracy" likely helps both ad suitability and RPM. Monetization requires YPP, which means 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours or 10M Shorts views (background knowledge, not verified here). So a November 2026 launch would see little ad revenue within 3 months unless it grows fast.

### Gaps
- I have no creator-disclosed RPM screenshots specific to conspiracy documentaries. AIR Media-Tech's July 2026 300-channel study ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/how-much-does-youtube-really-pay-in-2026-real-rpm-data-from-300-channels)) had no conspiracy row in the excerpt I saw.

## 5. Narravox: what it is, and the risks of AI story generators for "true story" content

### Takeaway
"Narravox AI" (narravoxai.com) is a small web tool for faceless creators. It generates stories, AI narration and scene prompts. Snippets say its genre choices are "True Life" and "Sci-Fi" with sub-genres. I could not confirm a continent or location selector, the maker or the pricing beyond "free and premium plans". ScamAdviser gives it a slightly low trust score. Using generated "true life" stories as factual documentaries carries a high risk of fabricated facts and falls into the narrated-story format that YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" update names.

### Cited Findings
- Narravox AI describes itself as an AI storytelling assistant that "helps you instantly generate stories, narration, and scene prompts for animated or faceless videos", with free and premium plans. Story genres are reportedly limited to True Life and Sci-Fi with sub-genres, and generated stories are converted into narration "with no recording needed". (Snippet only; the site could not be fetched.) — [Narravox AI](https://www.narravoxai.com/)
- ScamAdviser gives narravoxai.com a slightly low trust score and lists a Delaware address. — [ScamAdviser](https://www.scamadviser.com/check-website/narravoxai.com)
- A related YouTube channel, "Narravox AI" (@Narravox_Ai), exists. There is also a separate "Narravox" channel focused on "true horror and unsolved mysteries", but its link to the tool is unconfirmed. — [YouTube @Narravox_Ai](https://www.youtube.com/@Narravox_Ai); [YouTube Narravox channel](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCtyo8-X3GvrZzCQI-j-QPcA)
- **Name collisions:** a Qloo LLM Hackathon 2025 project on Devpost, "a collaborative storytelling platform crafting unique adventures from your interests"; a Quebec voice-over business (narravox.com, Chantal Girard); a YupVox TTS voice; a Kindle book series; a Spanish TikTok account with about 47 followers. — [Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/narravox); [narravox.com](https://narravox.com/); [YupVox](https://yupvox.com/voice/CL0641); [Amazon](https://us.amazon.com/dp/B0F4Q3YHW1); [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@narravox)
- **YouTube inauthentic-content update (effective 2025-07-15):** the YPP "repetitious content" policy was renamed and clarified to catch mass-produced and repetitive uploads. Penalties range from video demonetization to YPP removal and, in severe cases, suspension. Coverage says YouTube's examples include channels uploading narrated stories that differ only superficially, and slideshows reusing the same narration. AI use itself is not banned, and the definitions are widely called vague. — [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/); [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/may-see-less-ai-slop-214600306.html); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2025/07/10/youtube-monetization-policies-combating-ai-content/)
- **AI disclosure:** since March 2024, creators must tick a Studio checkbox for realistic altered or synthetic content that viewers could mistake for a real person, place, scene or event. YouTube may apply the label itself, and labels appear in the description or on the player. 2026 reports describe a unified label format with an appeal path, but details are thin and the sources conflict on dates. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/03/18/youtube-altered-content-disclosure/); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-introduces-mandatory-disclosure-for-ai-content/)

### Inferences
- An LLM-based "True Life" generator with no cited sources is very likely to invent names, dates, quotes and events. Presented as a documentary of real conspiracy events, that content can mislead viewers. Fabricating "historic events" sits close to YouTube's borderline and misinformation categories, and an exposed fabrication would damage the channel's reputation in a niche whose audience values debunking.
- Using the tool's narration and templated stories across many uploads matches the "narrated stories with superficial differences" example given in coverage of the July 2025 update. That is a direct YPP-rejection risk for a channel that needs fast monetization.
- AI-generated realistic scene visuals (for example, depicting a real event) probably require the altered/synthetic disclosure. An AI voice alone generally does not, per the "realistic" test, but that is not verified for 2026 rules.
- Safer use: treat Narravox, if used at all, as a drafting aid only. Do original research with citations, add human script editing, analysis and original visuals, and do not publish generated "true life" text as fact.

App-store searches returned no "Narravox" app. Fetching narravoxai.com requires adding it to the session's network allowlist.
- I could not retrieve the exact primary text of YouTube's inauthentic-content policy.
