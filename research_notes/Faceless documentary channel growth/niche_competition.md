## 1. Leading channels, their sizes, and fast-growing faceless launches (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
The niche is large and crowded at the top, with multi-million-subscriber networks and creator brands. I found **no independently verified case** of a 2024–2026 faceless, AI-voiced documentary channel reaching 100k subscribers within about 3 months. The claims I did find are self-reported or come from vendor blogs, and the most-cited one ("261k in 5 months") is publicly disputed. Vendor guides put a realistic 0→100k timeline at 8–14 months or more.

### Inferences

- Fast growth (100k in ≤3 months) is plausible as an outlier, but no verified 2024–2026 AI-voiced documentary example surfaced. The most visible fast AI successes were sleep-history content and fabricated true crime, and the latter was terminated.
- The supply of AI documentary content rose sharply in 2025–2026, which raises competition and policy risk for generic templates.

## 2. Which sub-topics pull views; saturated vs underserved

### Takeaway
Mainstream US true crime, especially famous serial killers, is widely described as oversaturated. International and non-English cases, lesser-known cases, and long-form "sleep" history are repeatedly named as gaps, but only by vendor or agency blogs with no supporting data. No source I found quantifies views by sub-topic.

### Cited Findings
- An agency selling faceless true crime calls Ted Bundy and Jeffrey Dahmer "oversaturated". It recommends lesser-known serial killers and lists true crime cases from other countries as underserved in English. — [White Glove Content](https://www.whiteglovecontent.com/niches/true-crime)
- A guide says international true crime cases have "almost no English-language YouTube coverage despite massive global search volume", without data. — [imagine.art](https://www.imagine.art/blogs/how-to-start-true-crime-youtube-channel)
- Real Stories' cartel content (about 5.8M-sub channel) suggests Latin American crime stories have a proven audience. — [White Glove Content](https://www.whiteglovecontent.com/niches/true-crime) (snippet)
- Cold case and unsolved content is positioned as a sub-niche with a lower RPM ($4–9) than general true crime. — [FluxNote cold case](https://fluxnote.io/guides/cold-case-youtube-channel); [FluxNote unsolved](https://fluxnote.io/blog/is-unsolved-cases-a-good-youtube-niche-in-2026-rpm-and-growth-data)
- Business documentaries (company rise-and-fall stories, scams) are covered as a distinct faceless format in a 2026 "teardown". I saw the title only. — [OutlierKit business docs](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-business-documentary-channels/)
- Fern's mix (unsolved crimes, hacking, intelligence, espionage) shows that tech, espionage and scam stories can scale to millions. — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/fern-tv/)
- The "dark history and untold stories" sub-niche is marketed as a faceless format. — [flowshorts](https://flowshorts.app/how-to-start-a-faceless-youtube-channel/history)
- Sleep or "boring" history is flooded with AI channels in 2025–2026 (see Section 1), so it is likely saturated. — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/people-are-going-to-sleep-while-listening-to-ai-generated-trash-on-youtube//amp/)

### Inferences
- Most saturated: famous US serial killers and murder cases, generic "top 10 mysteries", and AI sleep history.
- Likely underserved, based on weak evidence: well-researched non-Western cases (Asian, African, Latin American and Eastern European crimes, disasters and history), little-known disasters and engineering failures, scams and business fraud, and espionage. These could be exploited by channels that add real research, which also helps avoid the inauthentic-content rules.
- Financial scam and business-fraud topics likely pair higher-value ad audiences with lower advertiser-safety risk than violent crime.

### Gaps
- No search-volume or view-share data by sub-topic (survival, disasters, scams, dark history). Comparative claims rest on vendor opinion.
- Nothing on non-English-language documentary channels (for example, Spanish- or Hindi-language true crime) and their competition.

## 3. RPM/CPM ranges and ad suitability for sensitive topics

### Takeaway
Vendor estimates cluster around a **$5–12 RPM for long-form true crime and documentary**, with US-heavy audiences at about $10–12, and outlier claims of $15–25. Shorts earn pennies. YouTube's ad guidelines allow full monetization for factual or documentary coverage of violence and tragedy if it is non-graphic. Graphic focus brings limited ads, and in 2025 a separate "inauthentic content" rule began targeting templated AI narration.

### Cited Findings
- True crime RPM is about $5–12 (2026). US-heavy channels see about $10–12, and UK/AU about $7–9. An audit of 24 faceless true crime channels gave an average RPM of $10.1. A separate post's headline says "True Crime $9" versus Finance $50 and Gaming $3. All of these are FluxNote vendor figures with an unclear methodology. — [FluxNote true crime niche](https://fluxnote.io/blog/is-true-crime-a-good-youtube-niche-in-2026-rpm-and-growth-data); [FluxNote RPM by niche](https://fluxnote.io/blog/youtube-rpm-by-niche-2026); [FluxNote earnings](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-much-true-crime-channel-makes)
- Outlier claims: $18–25 RPM for true crime, a $12–22 CPM, and an $8–14 CPM citing "TubeVertex 2026". A FluxNote guide titled "7 Story YouTube Niches That Pay $15-$40 RPM". These conflict with the $5–12 consensus. — [FluxNote story niches](https://fluxnote.io/guides/best-niches-for-story-youtube-channels); [Autonolab](https://blog.autonolab.com/niches/2025-11-07-faceless-youtube-true-crime/)
- Cold cases are put at about $4–9 RPM. True crime Shorts earn about $0.03–0.12 RPM versus $4–10 for long-form. RPM is typically 40–60% of CPM. — [FluxNote cold case](https://fluxnote.io/guides/cold-case-youtube-channel); [FluxNote shorts](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-rpm-true-crime-niche)
- **YouTube's official policy** says content focused on blood, violence or injury "without other context" is not suitable for ads. Authoritative news or "documentary videos about a historic event" may be eligible, and non-graphic educational or historical depictions are treated differently. — [YouTube Help: advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- Secondary sources report that YouTube relaxed the rules (reported Jan 2026) so that non-graphic educational, news or documentary coverage of sensitive issues can earn full ads. I could not confirm the exact official wording. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-relaxes-monetization-controversial-content-2026/); [Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-news/youtube-ads-on-sensitive-topics); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-updates-ad-placement-rules-related-to-dramatic-content/809515)
- A third-party checklist says that even with context, these categories carry a "higher risk of limited monetization". — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-ad-friendly-content-checklist)
- **Inauthentic content policy (from Jul 15, 2025):** the "repetitious content" rule was renamed "inauthentic content" and targets mass-produced or repetitive content. YouTube's examples include channels that upload narrated stories with only superficial differences and slideshows with the same narration. The policy does not ban AI voices as such. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/7/youtube-updates-its-policy-to-demonetize-inauthentic-mass-produced-ai-generated-content); [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-has-creators-concerned-marketers-cheering); [logie.ai on 2026 crackdown](https://logie.ai/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026-monetization/)
- Fabricated "true" stories led to termination: True Crime Case Files, Feb 2025. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/14/youtube-true-crime-case-files-ai-crime-channel-terminated/)
- Anecdotal audience feedback: a creator said an AI voice is "rather off putting to some viewers". — search summary, no stable URL; treat as anecdotal

### Inferences
- For planning, a $4–10 RPM for long-form true-story content (lower for international audiences) is a defensible range. Treat figures above $15 as best-case or US-only.
- Sensitive topics are monetizable if they are non-graphic, factual and documentary-framed. Thumbnails and titles that sensationalize gore or violence raise the risk of limited ads.
- For AI-voiced channels, the bigger risk is the inauthentic-content and YPP review rather than ad suitability. Each video needs distinct, original research and editing, not a reused template. The facts must be real; fabrication has led to termination.

### Gaps
- No primary RPM data (for example, creator analytics screenshots or a YouTube-published figure). All ranges come from vendors.
- I could not verify the exact wording or date of the reported January 2026 sensitive-topics relaxation, or how the 2026 enforcement of the inauthentic-content policy has affected AI-narrated documentary channels.
